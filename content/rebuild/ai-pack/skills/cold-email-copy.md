# Cold email copy

Read this before writing any first-touch cold email, selecting a framework or generating variations. This file contains the writing rules and reusable frameworks. It needs no private workflow or template library.

## Inputs and approval

Read offer.md, research-brief.md, fit-rules.md and copy-rules.md when available. Confirm the offer, audience, actual capabilities, approved proof and useful deliverable. Missing evidence stays UNKNOWN. Draft useful copy from what is known; flag anything that prevents locking it. Never fill a proof gap with a plausible result.

Write one Email 1 first, identify its framework and get owner feedback. Save corrections as reusable rules in copy-rules.md. Build the wider pack after the direction is approved. Writing or locking copy does not authorize sending, starting a campaign, buying data or paying for generation. Obtain owner OK before spending money. This skill only drafts and saves copy.

## Always rules

- Exactly two cold steps: Email 1 offers help; Email 2 is a one-line bump in the same thread. Stop on any reply. Reply handling and approved booking follow-ups belong to the reply skills, outside this cold sequence.
- Subject is exactly `{{first_name}}`. Keep it fixed across variations. Email 2 inherits the thread subject; do not manufacture a new subject or a misleading reply prefix.
- Use `{{first_name}}` consistently. Never use a prospect company-name merge field. Say "you" or "your team". Never address the prospect as "your SDRs", "your recruiters" or another assumed team role. Buyer titles can describe the audience your offer helps them reach.
- No em dashes, contrast slogans, exclamation points, empty compliments or hype. Avoid constructions that pit one claim against another. Use periods, commas and ordinary sentences.
- Do not use "quick", "conversation" or "deal flow" in email copy. Do not describe an offer as "free" unless the owner explicitly approved that campaign offer. Do not invent discounts, guarantees, performance terms or trial availability.
- Email 1 must end with a concrete offer plus a meeting question containing "grabbing some time", "a brief intro" or "an intro". A request to send information alone is insufficient for Email 1. The final sentence is a question; no "Hey" in the closer.
- Use spoken, plain language. Read it aloud as a coworker note. Give enough context to understand the offer; cut slogans and unexplained industry shorthand. Do not force every email into a tiny word limit that removes context.
- Explain a product on first mention: "We built a tool called [Product] that [plain function]." Never assume recognition or open with unexplained usage instructions. For services, use "We help [buyer type] [outcome]" and explain the work plainly.
- Use researched pains appropriate to that persona. A hiring signal does not establish a budget, broken process or dissatisfaction.
- Signals are targeting only: hiring, job changes, funding, events, technology changes, social activity and other trigger data stay out of the email unless the owner explicitly approved a signal-referencing campaign. Public visibility alone is insufficient permission. Even then, verify the exact statement and keep its source and date outside the email.
- No invented results, testimonial people, customer names, market sizes or numbers. Use anonymous approved proof. This pack contains no real customer names. Never promote a possible use case into a proven result or guaranteed prospect outcome.
- Where approved testimonial outcomes exist, open some variants with the business result. Do not bury all proof in bullets. Without approved proof, use a plain capability opener.
- Avoid pronouns without a clear referent. Do not assume anyone's gender.
- Use only hyphen bullets. Capitalize each bullet. Put a blank line between the setup and the first bullet. Bullets describe three distinct outcomes or practical ideas; do not repeat the opener.
- Use plain text. Keep signatures minimal and approved. No tracking links, attachments or calendar links in first-touch copy by default. Never obscure domains to evade filters.

## Anatomy: what makes a reply worthwhile

A finished email answers five questions:
1. Why is this relevant? The pain and offer fit the segment, without exposing the targeting signal.
2. What do you actually do? State the function or service in ordinary words.
3. Why believe it? Give approved evidence when available. Specific mechanisms can explain an offer but cannot substitute for proof of results.
4. What do I get? Offer a workflow map, sample, checklist, demo or other deliverable the owner can actually provide.
5. What is the next step? Ask for an intro with a clear purpose. Reduce effort by explaining what will be covered and, if true, that the prospect can use the material internally.

Specificity means a real process, audience and deliverable. It does not require a numerical promise. Risk reversal must reflect terms the owner actually authorized. Do not promise implementation, migration or an asset already built when it does not exist.

## Two primary shapes

All names below are fictional product labels. Examples illustrate wording only; their capabilities and artifacts must be replaced with the owner's actual offer before use. Examples are never evidence.

### A. Three ideas

Start with two spoken sentences explaining the tool or service. Add a setup line and three distinct outcomes. End with an offer and intro ask. When proof is available, an approved outcome can lead the first paragraph, followed by the product explanation.

Email 1, subject `{{first_name}}`:

```text
{{first_name}},

We built a tool called Clearfolder that gathers supplier documents in one place. Your team can see what is missing before sending a supplier for approval.

We could probably help your team with:

- Giving each supplier a clear list of documents to provide
- Keeping renewal dates visible to everyone handling approvals
- Passing a complete review record to the next person

I can walk you through a sample approval checklist your team can use. Would it be worth grabbing some time?
```

Email 2, in the same thread:

```text
{{first_name}} - If it helps, I can send the checklist over first.
```

### B. Stealth offer

Lead with a researched pain question, explain the relevant tool or service, then close with an offer and meeting question. The opener describes a plausible process problem without asserting it exists at the recipient's company. A question opener plus a question closer is allowed; do not add more questions. For a one-question version, make the opener a plain pain statement grounded in research.

Email 1, subject `{{first_name}}`:

```text
{{first_name}},

How does your team keep supplier renewals from getting lost between reviews?

We built a tool called Clearfolder that tracks document expiry dates and collects replacement files in one place. Your team can review the outstanding items before the next approval.

I can show you a sample renewal workflow to use with your team. Would you be open to a brief intro?
```

Email 2, in the same thread:

```text
{{first_name}} - Happy to send the workflow outline if that is easier.
```

Outcome-led variation: replace the opening with `[Approved anonymous result, including measurement period and scope].` Follow with the same plain explanation of the product. Use the wording of the evidence and avoid implying the same result is guaranteed for the prospect. If there is no proof, keep the capability-led version.

## Framework picker

Frameworks supply structure. Every framework still follows the rules above, the fixed subject and the two-step limit. Choose based on available evidence and delivery capacity, rather than forcing a dramatic hook.

| Framework | When to use | What must exist |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lead generation core | A service connects buyers with a defined market | Approved ICP and actual service scope |
| Market map / TAM | Market coverage itself is useful | Dated, deduplicated counts with clear coverage limits |
| Anonymous case study | A relevant outcome can be documented | Approved metric, baseline, period and anonymous descriptor |
| Competitor alternative | Replacing an established workflow or category | Verified capabilities and approved comparison; no assumed dissatisfaction |
| Done-for-you system | Owner manages a recurring process | Defined delivery boundaries and owner responsibilities |
| Data / research | An actual finding informs a business decision | Source, date, sample and limitations |
| Playbook / strategy | A useful method can be taught | Shareable artifact or approved commitment to create it |
| Direct question | One researched process pain fits the persona | Evidence of segment relevance |
| Automation | A specific task can be automated | Verified inputs, outputs and human review boundaries |
| Prepared sample / pilot | An artifact exists or a bounded pilot is approved | Real asset or approved scope and terms |
| Follow-up / routing | No reply after Email 1 | Same thread and no suppression or reply; step 2 only |

### Ten complete Email 1 skeletons

Subject for every skeleton: `{{first_name}}`. Square brackets are instructions to the writer, not merge fields. Resolve them with supported facts before locking. Each skeleton ends with an offer and an approved meeting phrase. Pair each with one bump from the pool below to make exactly two steps.

#### 1. Lead generation core
```text
{{first_name}},

We help [B2B business type] get in front of [buyer titles] at [target company type]. We handle [actual list and outreach work] around the accounts your team wants to reach.

I can walk you through a sample account plan your team can also use internally. Would you be open to a brief intro?
```

#### 2. Market map
```text
{{first_name}},

Our market map covers [verified count] [target account type] in [geography], based on [source and date]. We help your team turn that research into a prioritized account list, with coverage gaps marked.

I can show you a sample of the map and how we organize the accounts. Would it be worth grabbing some time?
```

#### 3. Anonymous case study with outcome opener
```text
{{first_name}},

We helped a [approved anonymous business descriptor] [verified result] over [measured period]. Our team handled [actual work], with [relevant scope or measurement limit].

I can walk you through the case study and share the steps your team could assess. Would you be open to an intro?
```

#### 4. Competitor alternative
```text
{{first_name}},

How does your team handle [researched process friction] in your current setup?

We built a tool called [Product] that [verified function]. It supports [verified alternative workflow], with [actual migration or onboarding scope].

I can show you a side-by-side workflow outline for your team to evaluate. Would you be open to a brief intro?
```
Use the competitor category by default. Naming the recipient's installed tool exposes a targeting signal and requires explicit campaign approval plus verified usage. Never claim their vendor is failing or promise a contract buyout without authorized terms.

#### 5. Done-for-you system
```text
{{first_name}},

We run [defined process] for [business type]. Our team handles [actual tasks], and your team reviews [specific handoff or approval].

I can walk you through a sample delivery plan, including what your team would need to provide. Would it be worth grabbing some time?
```

#### 6. Data / research insight
```text
{{first_name}},

Our analysis of [actual dataset] found [supported finding] during [period]. The sample covers [scope], so we use it to inform [relevant decision].

We help teams [actual service tied to that decision]. I can share the breakdown and explain its limits. Would you be open to a brief intro?
```

#### 7. Playbook / strategy
```text
{{first_name}},

We help [business type] [supported outcome or capability] by [plain mechanism]. We have a playbook covering [actual topics] that your team could use to plan the work.

I can walk you through the playbook and share the checklist for your team to keep. Would you be open to an intro?
```

#### 8. Direct question / stealth
```text
{{first_name}},

How is your team handling [researched persona-specific process problem]?

We help [business type] [relevant service]. That includes [plain explanation of how it works].

I can show you a sample workflow and explain where it may fit. Would it be worth grabbing some time?
```

#### 9. Task automation
```text
{{first_name}},

We built a tool called [Product] that [specific task] using [supported input]. Your team reviews [human decision] before [next action].

I can show you a sample run and the review checklist your team would use. Would you be open to a brief intro?
```
Do not turn automation into an unsupported claim about staffing reductions or savings.

#### 10. Prepared sample / bounded pilot
```text
{{first_name}},

We put together a [actually completed sample] showing [specific function]. We help [business type] [plain service or outcome], and the sample shows how that work is structured.

I can walk you through it and share the outline for your team to assess. Would you be open to an intro?
```
If nothing has been built, change the first sentence to an honest capability statement. A pilot alternative must name an owner-approved scope and evaluation criterion; never promise to launch it in the email.

## Email 1 CTA pool

Use deliverables the owner can fulfill. Match the offer to the body. Rotate whole closers, with the meeting question at the end:

- I can walk you through a sample workflow your team can keep. Would it be worth grabbing some time?
- I can show you the checklist and explain how the handoff works. Would you be open to a brief intro?
- I can share the research breakdown and help your team assess the findings. Would you be open to an intro?
- I can walk you through the account plan, including how to use it internally. Would it be worth grabbing some time?
- I can show you a sample review and leave you with the template. Would you be open to a brief intro?
- I can share the case study and walk through what may apply to your team. Would you be open to an intro?
- I can show you how the sample works and send the outline afterward. Would it be worth grabbing some time?
- I can explain the delivery plan and share the checklist for your team to assess. Would you be open to a brief intro?

Do not substitute a vague invitation or a standalone request to send information for these Email 1 endings.

## Step 2 bump pool

Choose one per variant. Entire body is one line: `{{first_name}} - [bump]`. Keep the first name, hyphen and text together. No product recap, fresh proof, new pitch, separate greeting, signature paragraph or third step. Offer only an asset that exists or can be provided as approved.

- `{{first_name}} - Hey, just let me know if I can send the one-pager over.`
- `{{first_name}} - If it is easier, I can send the workflow outline first.`
- `{{first_name}} - Happy to share the checklist if that would help.`
- `{{first_name}} - Hey, just bumping this. Are you the right person on the team for this?`
- `{{first_name}} - Would you like the sample to review with your team?`
- `{{first_name}} - I can send the outline over if that is useful.`

Routing and warm bumps belong here. Do not bolt on the library's longer follow-up sequences, extra case studies or pressure-based breakup emails.

## Variation and fingerprinting

Create 10 to 15 or more owner-approved Email 1 variants across at least the two primary shapes when the offer supports both. Use different relevant pains, outcome order, proof placement, explanatory sentences, cadence, bullet versus paragraph structure and offer-led closers. The ten skeletons provide additional angles; use only those with supporting inputs.

Do not create variants by swapping a greeting or a few synonyms. Similar bodies can remain recognizable to recipients and mail systems. Variation supports useful testing; it does not guarantee delivery or justify evading filtering. Keep the subject fixed even though other libraries recommend subject rotation.

Spintax only on owner request. Confirm the sequencer's current syntax and render every branch before loading. Never let a swap change a claim, introduce an unsupported promise or break grammar. Do not assume syntax transfers between sequencers.

## Personalization and AI variables

Use verified website content to choose business categories, customer types and relevant use cases. Personalization should improve the offer's relevance, rather than narrate how the prospect was found. An optional `{{buyer}}` or `{{use_case}}` must have a documented source, approved output format and tested fallback.

Use enrichment-variables for calibration: hand-write 10 to 15 outputs on actual rows, obtain owner approval, and embed 3 or 4 approved input/output examples in the production prompt. Never treat this skill's fictional examples as approved campaign proof. Every prompt includes website content. A signal-derived field stays internal unless signal references were explicitly approved for that campaign.

If content is missing, ambiguous or contradictory, mark it for review. Use an owner-approved generic variant only when its offer still fits; otherwise hold the lead. Do not invent a buyer, pain, metric or artifact to fill a blank.

## Lock and handoff

Before locking, check every variant for subject, two-step structure, plain product context, persona fit, distinct bullets, sourced proof, permitted CTA, signal policy and unresolved fields. Render at least five complete examples across different leads and edge cases. Read them aloud. Check every approved variant and every spintax branch.

When the owner says "lock", save copy-locked.md containing:
- Version and approval date; audience, offer and approved signal-reference policy.
- Every Email 1 verbatim, labeled A, B, C and so on, with its framework.
- Approved one-line Email 2 options; only one executes for each lead.
- Fixed subject `{{first_name}}`, same-thread behavior and owner-approved delay (default proposal: three business days).
- Fields, source columns, prompt versions, fallbacks and hold conditions.
- Evidence references and asset availability for every claim and offer.

Hand off to sequencer-uploader. That skill must run lead-list-cleaner and sending-domain-blacklist before loading. Copy approval never starts a campaign.
