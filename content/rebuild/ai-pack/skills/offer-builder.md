# Offer builder

## When to use
- After market-research, before cold-email-copy.
- When the owner wants to sell a new offer or lead with a new trigger.

## Steps
1. Read research-brief.md. If it is missing, ask the owner to run market-research first.
2. Pick the trigger where buyers are already spending money. Say in one line why you picked it.
3. Write the core sentence: who we help and the result they get. Keep targeting triggers internal unless the owner approved a signal-referencing campaign. It must be readable aloud in under ten seconds.
4. Write the reply ask as a useful offer plus a meeting question using "grabbing some time", "a brief intro" or "an intro". Offer a sample, checklist or walkthrough the owner can deliver.
5. Check every claim against what the owner can prove. Cut anything they cannot back up.
6. Give the owner two versions of the sentence. Ask them to pick one or ask for new ones.
7. When the owner says "approved", save offer.md with: core sentence, targeting trigger (with link and signal-reference policy), who it is for (titles and company types), reply ask, proof we may mention, claims we never make.

## Rules
- Plain words. Describe the result the buyer gets in their terms.
- No invented results, numbers or client names. Only proof the owner approved.
- Do not write emails yet.

## Example core sentences (made up)
- We help regional food makers get in front of grocery chains that are adding private-label lines this year.
- We help industrial suppliers organize product documents for distributor reviews.

## What good output looks like
- One sentence a stranger understands on first read.
- A trigger with a source link.
- An offer-led ask such as "I can walk you through a sample account plan. Would you be open to a brief intro?"

## Authorization and privacy

Never send email, start a campaign or spend money without owner OK for the action or an explicit written scope. Existing approval applies only within that scope; silence is not approval. Store credentials through secure connections and keep keys, tokens and account identifiers out of files and reports.
