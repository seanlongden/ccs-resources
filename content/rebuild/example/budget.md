# Completed budget example
Pack: 1.0.0. Illustrative numbers, not a purchase recommendation or quote. Check actual plans, taxes, limits and billing periods before spending.

| Item | Example basis | Monthly cost allocation |
|---|---|---:|
| Grok Bot entitlement | $30 planning allowance; actual eligible checkout must be confirmed | $30 |
| Sequencer | Example $107 monthly plan allowance | $107 |
| Data | Example $49 monthly allowance | $49 |
| Sending inboxes | 20 inboxes at $3.50, including reserve group | $70 |
| Domains | 10 at $12/year, $120 paid annually | $10 |
| Calendar | $120 paid annually | $10 |
| Optional model/crawl sample budget | Separate $20 cap only if actually needed | $20 |
| Total example monthly cost | Includes annual bills divided across 12 months | $296 |

Twenty sending inboxes do not mean twenty active senders at once. The example reserves a second group. Sending limits and readiness still need checking. A separate model/crawl account is not automatically required just because Grok Bot is used.

The $10 calendar allocation is an optional paid-plan example, not a required API cost. Calendly's 3 November 2026 update makes Scheduling API and standard webhooks available on Free, subject to scopes, feature access and rate limits. If Free meets your needs, remove the $120 annual calendar bill: the example allocation becomes $286/month and first-month tool cash becomes $396.

If the calendar and domains are paid annually at the start, first-month cash in this example is $276 of monthly bills plus $240 annual bills = $516. That is different from the $296 monthly cost allocation.

For an illustrative $3,000 upfront agency payment, $516 initial tool cash would leave $2,484 before labour, tax and other delivery costs. This is not profit. Add real operator time, any caller quote and all other delivery costs before setting a real engagement price.

Research sample example: a 50-company run costing $2.50 would be $0.05 per company. A 1,000-company forecast would be $50 for that stage alone. These are invented arithmetic inputs for teaching; use actual provider usage and approve a cap before a real run.
