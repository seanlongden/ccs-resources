# Worked Example: When Another Agency Charges Less

**How do you explain a higher fee when a buyer thinks every provider sells the same meeting?** Follow the decisions below, then use the workbook for your own agency.

This is an **illustrative scenario**, prompted by the cheaper-provider and internal-hire questions raised in CCS coaching. The buyer quotes, agency capabilities, costs and proposed package below are invented for teaching. They are not Andrei's verified offer or a client success story.

## 1. Start With What the Buyer Says

Our example agency opens with: “We book qualified meetings for B2B service businesses.” The prospect replies: “Another provider charges less per meeting. Why would we pay you more?”

Instead of defending the price, the agency asks about the buyer's experience and what they are comparing.

| Example buyer evidence | What we learn |
| --- | --- |
| “The last provider filled the calendar, but too many companies were too small for us.” | Fit matters more than appointment count alone. |
| “I still had to sort the replies and check what each person wanted.” | The founder wants less work around the appointments. |
| “We are comparing your service with another agency and hiring someone.” | Both are relevant alternatives. Doing nothing is also possible. |
| “If a suitable buyer shows up, I can handle the sales call.” | The founder wants acquisition handled but accepts responsibility for selling. |

**Decision:** Investigate a service for founders who can sell but need suitable conversations without supervising the work before the call. Do not assume that all B2B businesses have this problem.

**What remains unknown:** The other provider's scope, what the founder means by “too small”, their sales economics and whether our agency can deliver enough suitable opportunities.

## 2. Translate the Problem Into Delivery Options

The agency checks its current scope and samples of completed work. In this teaching scenario, it already builds lists, writes campaigns, handles replies and screens companies. It does not consistently document why a meeting fits.

| Obstacle | Possible response | Status in this example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unsuitable companies reach the calendar. | Agree observable fit criteria before launch, then screen against them. | Screening exists; criteria need a clearer written agreement. |
| The founder still manages interested replies. | Explain that reply handling through booking is included. | Already delivered; currently hidden in the pitch. |
| The founder does not know what to expect on the call. | Supply the relevant reply and a brief fit note with each booking. | New delivery improvement to test. |
| The founder doubts agency claims. | Show comparable outcomes and sample delivery records. | Check the actual evidence held before claiming results. |

**Why this matters:** Some of the answer is better communication. Some requires changing the service. Rewording the existing pitch cannot create missing proof or a delivery process.

Checking fit does not prove purchase intent. The agency must explain what it checks, what the prospect said and what is still unknown. It should not relabel every screened contact as a ready-to-buy lead.

## 3. Compare the Actual Alternatives

The agency asks what the other quote includes. Until it has that answer, it records “unknown”, not “they only send leads”.

If the other provider includes the same screening, reply handling and meeting context, those items are not differences between the providers. The agency must look at other relevant evidence or accept that its current premium is not justified for this buyer.

An internal hire might give the buyer more control. It can also mean recruiting, training and supervising someone. Ask what the founder would actually take on; do not assume a hire is always slower or more expensive.

For the rest of this illustrative exercise, assume the buyer shares the competing quote and it excludes managing interested replies. That creates a confirmed scope difference within our fictional scenario. We still do not know which provider would generate better results.

**Decision:** Explain the included reply handling to this buyer, because they specifically want that work removed. Do not claim that every competing agency excludes it. If the actual quote included it, this reason for a premium would disappear.

## 4. Choose What to Keep and What to Reject

The agency keeps the reply-handling work and improves the fit agreement. It considers adding a short booking brief. It also considers offering recruitment, team training and system ownership, then rejects those additions for this buyer: the founder has asked for the work to be run, not a team to manage.

### Check the Cost Before Adding the Brief

Illustrative monthly assumptions:

- Fee: £3,000.
- Existing direct delivery cost, including delivery labour: £1,400.
- Briefs: 20 appointments × 10 minutes = 3 hours 20 minutes.
- Labour for briefs at £30/hour: £100.
- Quality checks and tools for the addition: £100.

The new direct delivery cost is £1,600. That leaves £1,400, or about 46.7% of the fee, **before general overheads, sales costs and tax**. It is not net profit or a recommended margin.

The agency would compare this with its own required margin and capacity. It should measure the time during a pilot; if each brief takes much longer, the calculation changes. Do not copy the fee or volume into your own offer.

**Decision:** Test a brief based on information already collected. Reject an exhaustive bespoke research report for every appointment unless its added value and cost are justified.

For this example, assume the agency requires at least a 40% direct-delivery margin and has five hours of monthly capacity for the addition. The estimate meets that margin and uses 3 hours 20 minutes before quality checks. The delivery lead must verify total time, completeness and whether the client can prepare from the brief. If checks push it beyond five hours, simplify the brief or change the price before including it routinely. These thresholds are illustrative business choices, not universal benchmarks.

## 5. Build the Claim and Evidence Together

| Intended statement | What would support it |
| --- | --- |
| “We handle replies through to booking.” | The agreed scope and samples showing the process is performed. |
| “You can see why each appointment fits.” | Agreed criteria and completed booking briefs. |
| “We have produced suitable sales conversations for businesses like yours.” | Relevant, permissioned results with dates and a clear definition of suitable. Without these, omit the claim. |
| “You only need a few hours a week.” | Measured client workload. We do not have it in this scenario, so we do not use that claim. |

A sample brief proves what the handover contains. It does not prove a meeting volume or sales outcome. That is why the example below avoids invented performance promises.

## 6. A Completed Positioning Brief

This is the filled-in equivalent of workbook page 5. It records both the supported position and the improvement still being tested.

| Brief field | Completed illustrative answer |
| --- | --- |
| Buyer, trigger and desired result | B2B service founder whose last provider generated poor-fit appointments and left them managing replies. Wants suitable sales conversations with less work before the call. |
| Alternative and obstacle | The shared competing quote excludes reply management. Internal hiring remains an option. Founder wants that work handled, not another role to supervise. |
| Chosen difference and buyer value | Included reply handling through booking removes work this buyer would otherwise do. This is a scope difference against this quote, not an exclusive agency capability. |
| Delivery and responsibilities | Agency handles targeting, campaigns, screening and replies. Founder approves offer and criteria, provides availability, attends and closes. Written fit criteria need improvement; booking briefs are a pilot until verified. |
| Claim, evidence and limits | Scope and delivery records support reply handling. Agreed criteria and samples support what screening checks. No comparative performance or client-revenue claim is established. |
| Unproven assumptions and next action | Test brief usefulness and total delivery time. Compare hiring if it remains a serious option. Gather permissioned relevant result evidence before making performance claims. |

**Current usable position:** We handle the interested replies the founder would otherwise manage under the competing quote. **Proposed improvement:** Clearer fit agreements and a booking brief. Keeping those separate prevents a useful idea becoming a false claim.

## 7. Write the Explanation

The wording below assumes the service changes have been tested and included in the agreed scope. Before then, describe them as a proposal or pilot.

### Short Introduction

> We run outbound for B2B service founders who can close suitable opportunities but do not want to manage prospecting and replies. We agree which companies fit, run the campaigns and handle responses through booking. Each appointment comes with the relevant conversation and a fit note, so you can prepare for the call without piecing the history together yourself.

The result is easier to picture than “full-service lead generation”. It also states which work the agency takes on. It still needs relevant proof during the conversation.

### First Outreach Message

Here is a hypothetical message for this buyer group, using the established reply-handling scope rather than the untested booking brief:

> Hi Sam, we run outbound for B2B service founders and handle interested replies through to booking, so you can take the sales calls without managing the inbox. Is handling those replies something you would want taken off your plate?

This tests whether the work matters to that audience. It does not assume Sam has the exact problems in our example, claim results we do not have or say research has already been prepared. Add relevant proof when you actually hold it.

### When They Say the Other Agency Is Cheaper

> Let's check what you would be comparing. You said the main problems were unsuitable companies and having to manage the replies yourself. Our scope includes screening against the criteria we agree, managing replies through booking and giving you the conversation context before the call. Does the other proposal cover those parts too? If it does, we should compare the evidence and terms as well. I wouldn't ask you to pay more just because we describe the same service differently.

The agency ties the difference to this buyer's concerns and checks the comparison. It does not attack a supplier it has not assessed. If the buyer only needs basic appointment setting, this wider offer may be unnecessary.

### Proposal Summary

> **The aim:** Suitable sales conversations without your team running the outreach and reply process.
>
> **Our responsibility:** Agree fit criteria, build and run campaigns, manage replies through booking, and supply the relevant conversation and fit note for each appointment.
>
> **Your responsibility:** Approve the offer and criteria, supply accurate business information, provide calendar availability, attend the calls and handle sales follow-up.
>
> **Evidence and terms:** Include only verified case evidence and the actual fee, scope, cancellation rules and any agreed guarantee. This exercise does not establish a meeting-volume or revenue guarantee.

For a cold email, use the most relevant reason to engage and an honest next step. Do not paste the entire proposal or claim to have prepared research you have not done.

## 8. Check the Response and Adapt

Ask the next suitable prospect what they think is included and what they would still need to handle. Record their answer and the next step, not just whether they say it sounds good.

If they still compare only appointment prices, find out why. They might not understand the scope, might already have reply handling covered, or might value the same service from another provider. Those require different responses.

**The completed decision:** Target a buyer who values the work before the sales call; explain existing reply handling; improve fit agreement and booking context; test delivery cost; use only supported evidence. Do not add training or ownership just to make the offer look bigger.

### Apply It to a Different Service

For a content agency, the obstacle might be the founder having to invent topics and rewrite scripts. Investigate whether research and scripting remove valuable work, whether you already do them and what proof you have. The same reasoning applies; the meeting package does not.

**Now complete your own brief.** If your evidence points to a different concern, follow that concern. The point is to learn the decision process, not copy this agency's offer.
